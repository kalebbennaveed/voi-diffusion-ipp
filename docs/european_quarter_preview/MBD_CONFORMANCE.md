# MBD trajectory optimization

The route-restricted v1–v5 recordings are archived. They are not evidence for
MBD trajectory optimization. Their trajectories and percentages must not be relabeled as
results of this implementation.

## Decision and rollout

The method name is **MBD**, as in Pan et al. The paper defines the trajectory as
Y = (x_1, ..., x_H, u_0, ..., u_{H-1}). Section 4.2 and Algorithm 2, line 4,
generate dynamically feasible state sequences by rolling out the control part
of each sample. Thus the states are determined by the controls and initial
state; they are not independently drawn paths. The official model-only code
likewise samples control sequences and obtains the state trajectories by rollout.
This is the paper's shooting formulation of trajectory optimization, not a
separate MBD variant or a restriction to a route library.

For horizon T and interval dt, H = T/dt. The decision has exactly 2H coordinates:
theta = (theta_0, ..., theta_{H-1}), theta_i in R². Every interval has two
independent coordinates. The commanded nominal acceleration is

    a_i = a_max tanh(theta_i)

elementwise. This smooth coordinate map enforces acceleration bounds without
clipping Gaussian samples. It is an application parameterization, not an
equation prescribed by MBD. The bounded parameter domain is [-12,12]^(2H).
Out-of-domain samples get zero target weight. The bounded domain makes a finite
cost's Boltzmann target normalizable. There are no route indices, inspection
orders, dwell variables, waypoint coordinates, spline control points or truncated
bases in this decision vector. RRT paths can initialize and guide the search;
they do not constrain the subsequent independent control coordinates. There is
no post-optimization path smoothing.

Nominal states follow the exact constant-acceleration double-integrator step.
Each performance-field rollout starts from the same measured state and clarity.
At each reached state it evaluates position/velocity feedback about that
nominal reference, learned mean-wind compensation, and (for the aware methods)
the state-dependent soft constraint filter. RK4 integrates the resulting
disturbed planar dynamics. Only the reached positions produce sensing and
clarity updates. The physical field is unavailable to the controller.

The cost is the integral of mean positive clarity deficit, plus separately
reported squared clearance and speed violation penalties. Jerk penalty defaults
to zero. There is no information bonus, route bonus, progress-to-selected-goal
reward, prescribed inspection sequence, or favorable multiplier on reported
performance. The reported physical mission cost is monitoring deficit alone.

## MBD equations retained

Source: Pan et al., *Model-Based Diffusion for Trajectory Optimization*,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01573, Eqs. (6), (8), (9), Algorithm 2.

At reverse level i, with cumulative alpha abar_i and one-step alpha_i:

    phi_i(theta | y_i) = N(y_i / sqrt(abar_i), (1/abar_i - 1) I)
    weighted_theta = sum_k w_k theta_k / sum_k w_k
    score_i = (-y_i + sqrt(abar_i) weighted_theta) / (1-abar_i)
    y_(i-1) = (y_i + (1-abar_i) score_i) / sqrt(alpha_i)

The state trajectory is generated by rollout, as in Algorithm 2. Eq. (10d)
can average the resulting state-and-control trajectories. Its control coordinates
are exactly the weighted control average; sampled state coordinates are replaced
by dynamics rollout at the next iteration. Omitting those overwritten state
coordinates does not restrict the set of trajectories. In this application,
the nominal decision coordinates are used in the score. Filtered inputs and
disturbed execution states are outcomes of evaluating a nominal trajectory, not
replacement decision coordinates.

The implementation uses the index in the derivation of Eq. (8). The printed
Algorithm 2's sampling line uses an inconsistent preceding-level index; mixing
that line with Eq. (9)'s current-level score would change the estimator.

## Explicit application additions and finite-budget choices

- The user's updated PDF supplies the lower-tail objective and archive proposal.
  Those are additions to MBD, not claims about the original paper.
- For posterior-probability performance samples, sort the costs and integrate
  the lowest eta fraction, including fractional mass of the last sample.
- Compute archive priorities against the evaluated expected-cost incumbent.
  Keep the archive fixed within a sampled batch. The actual proposal is
  q=(1-rho)phi+rho*sum_a nu_a N(theta_a,sigma_i²I).
- Use log weight -J_optimistic/temperature + log(phi) - log(q), including the
  entire mixture denominator. At rho=0 this reduces exactly to ordinary MBD.
- The user requested a cosine schedule. Record every alpha and the endpoint.
  The interval endpoint is recorded per run. The 30-second RRT-guided runs use
  [0,0.08], and the 45-second trials use [0,0.05]; earlier
  model-only runs used [0,0.65]. These are **not** the full
  cosine interval to nearly zero cumulative alpha. That endpoint is a numerical
  setting still requiring sensitivity checks; it must not be described as an
  exact terminal Gaussian approximation or a proven sampler of the target.
- Model-only cold initialization can be standard normal (Algorithm 2).
  RRT-guided calls forward-noise the best evaluated initial sequence, including
  the shifted previous decision. The unplanned tail is zero
  nominal acceleration; all its coordinates remain free in the next search.
- Zero-control and braking sequences are evaluated as fallback incumbents.
  They contain no environment geometry or inspection-site information.
- If every batch weight is zero, the score is undefined. Log the event and
  restart at an already evaluated incumbent at the next level. No fabricated
  score or normalized weights are reported for that event.
- Evaluate the final denoised vector. Reevaluate the leading archive vectors,
  expected-cost incumbent, previous sequence and final vector using a fresh,
  common validation bank. Selecting a best evaluated vector is an MPC selection
  step after MBD; it is not Algorithm 2's unqualified return of its final vector.
- Construction fields used by the filter are separate from performance and
  validation fields. Optimism never removes adverse construction fields.
- The `common_construction` experiment reuses the first call's base random draws
  for construction fields as the posterior changes. This is a variance-reduction
  choice for the finite-sample filter, not access to the physical field. Performance
  and validation fields retain their own fresh draws. The first planning call is
  unchanged. The setting is recorded, and all compared methods receive it.
- POAM updates use actual simulated observations between replans. There are no
  hypothetical posterior updates inside prediction in this revised method.

The official upstream implementation (commit
c1eb913783f1713f7a1ebb657b34e0188d52cd8b) also has practical choices, including
clipped proposals and batch-standardized rewards. This rebuild uses the paper's
Eq. (8) and fixed-temperature target instead of silently inheriting those choices.

## RRT guidance: Section 4.3 and Eq. (13)

The paper uses RRT demonstrations in its obstacle-rich Car2d example.
Here RRT-Connect searches the collision geometry from the measured position to
each inspection-region center. It receives no physical wind field, future
observations, preferred passage, method-specific route or prescribed circulation.
All compared methods use the same RRT generation procedure and seed rule.
Reached states differ during execution, so later generated paths can differ.

The `rrt_visits` configuration also allows multi-site initializations for
persistent monitoring. Single-visit paths remain available. For longer paths,
subsequent destinations are sampled from the other task regions and connected by
new RRT searches. All initial sequences are evaluated by the task objective.
The sampled visit order is recorded for auditing; it is not a constraint or a
discrete decision variable in MBD. A 45-second trial uses up to three visits to
test whether single-destination initialization was limiting return-trip planning.

Each geometric path is converted to a dynamically feasible double-integrator
trajectory using time-scaled quintic segments. The sampled accelerations initialize
the full control sequence. Stops at the RRT corners belong only to initialization;
MBD can change every acceleration at every 0.1-second interval. The output flown
is the independently evaluated chosen control sequence, not a smoothed RRT path.

Let P(theta) be the nominal position sequence, theta_d the best evaluated initial
sequence and J_d its task cost including the same soft constraint penalties.
The demonstration likelihood is a peak-normalized Gaussian:

    log L_d(theta) = -mean_h ||P_h(theta)-P_h(theta_d)||² / (2 sigma_d²)
    log w = max(-J(theta)/temperature,
                -J_d/temperature + log L_d(theta)) + log(phi) - log(q)

This is the model/demo maximum of Eq. (13), combined with the user's archive
proposal importance correction. For the stacked position vector, the Gaussian
covariance is (H+1)*sigma_d² I; sigma_d is an RMS position scale in metres. The
demonstration is fixed within a planning call. Raw task costs are not replaced by
this guide likelihood. Final trajectory selection uses only the task objective
on a fresh common validation bank, without a demonstration bonus.

Both RRT and MBD computation times are logged. The 30-second configuration has
300 intervals and 600 independent coordinates. Its cosine endpoint 0.08 is a
local refinement setting around evaluated initial trajectories, not a claim to
sample a globally optimal path distribution.

## Nonlinear execution

The planar controller/filter runs at 10 Hz from the actual state. The same
specific-force command goes through flatness-based attitude construction,
geometric attitude feedback, motor allocation, motor dynamics and nonlinear
rigid-body integration at 100 Hz. Position, attitude and sensing are read from
that integration. Horizontal feedback and wind compensation are applied once.
The piecewise-constant outer command can jump at sample boundaries; the physical
attitude/motor response is retained rather than replacing it with a smooth drawn
curve. Altitude feedback tracks 3.2 m. No extra barrier controller is introduced.

The wind field is synthetic spatial air velocity, not CFD. The posterior table
interpolates that field belief. There is no claim of hardware validation or
real-time planning. Compilation and planning wall time are recorded separately
from simulated mission time.

## Evidence

- `tests/quarter/test_full_control_mbd.py` compares Eqs. (6), (8), (9) with
  independent NumPy calculations and reconstructs every recorded reverse step.
- `tests/quarter/test_rrt_guidance.py` checks collision-free RRT segments,
  double-integrator initialization, free control coordinates and Eq. (13) weights
  against independent calculations. Guidance does not alter recorded task costs.
- The full control Jacobian is diagonal and full rank: perturbing any time
  coordinate changes its acceleration and subsequent nominal states.
- `tests/quarter/test_full_execution.py` compares compiled sampled-data execution
  against an independent NumPy integration of the nonlinear plant.
- `scripts/run_full_control_quarter.py` saves the complete configuration, source
  checksums, every chosen vector and the first call's raw samples, costs, weights,
  scores, reverse states, field banks and random key. `--audit-all` retains every
  planning call in that form.
- Numerical correctness does not establish task success. A mission that stays in
  one inspection site has not demonstrated inter-region transfer or a long-term
  advantage of learning. A planning-only diagnostic cannot establish mission
  behavior in either direction. Development failures remain in the recorded evidence.
