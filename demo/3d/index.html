What is measured, and what is illustrative?

The useful task shown here is repeatedly surveying two floodplain areas to keep a surface-water map current. Wetlands, riverbank details and gauge posts provide a visual setting. The retained experiment models a generic environmental quantity through uncertainty and clarity; it does not simulate water depth, flooding or real camera imagery. No water observations or flood-monitoring results have been invented.

Clarity and its deficit, POAM estimates, horizontal trajectories and MBD proposals come from the original recordings. The translucent red footprint shows the actual Gaussian sensing information weight H²/R, normalized to its value at the aircraft. The fill fades with sensing strength. The solid ring marks 50% weight; a faint outer ring marks 10%. Sensitivity decays smoothly beyond these rings. The downward view is rendered from a camera at the aircraft's recorded position; terrain occlusion in that image is cosmetic and is not an added sensing model.

Survey bounds are outlined, not filled by default. The north A and south B rectangles are the original positive-target cells; the ground-layer menu enables quantitative field overlays. The top-left map always shows the recorded whole-field clarity, with a Clarity / Deficit switch, shared scales, and the current sensing footprint. Q-driven information loss continues between visits. Corner wind maps always show the earned POAM posterior: arrows are mean acceleration disturbance, and the background shows component standard deviation. Physical wind can be selected separately in the landscape for diagnosis.

Orange arrows at the aircraft show wind push reconstructed from recorded motion and control, including drag. Blue arrows show control effort; the panel reports drag and net acceleration separately. Moving streaks and windsocks use the physical reference field to show wind direction, not calibrated airspeed. They animate even when mission playback is paused; Wind flow toggles that animation. Bank and pitch are illustrative. This is a 3D presentation of a planar simulation. The two pump-station buildings stay within the recorded conservative obstacle envelopes and rise above the fixed sensing altitude. Low pipework and maintenance paths connect them to the river; these are scenery below the aircraft. Scattered alpine chalets, barns and winding tracks across the wider hillsides provide settlement context and are not additional modeled obstacles. There is no vertical control. This replay does not enforce fixed-wing minimum airspeed, lift or turn-radius constraints; the fixed-wing airframe is a visualization of the existing paths. The airframe and the terrain are visual models; physical clearance comes from recorded dynamics. Forecasts use a sampled planning world (or still-air nominal motion for the wind-blind comparator). Both executions use wind-aware RPCBF. This is one development seed, not statistical validation.
